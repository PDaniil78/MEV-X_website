Every Chain Handles MEV Differently — One Decided to Keep It Inside

Ethereum, Solana, and Sui each solve MEV at the chain level in their own way. Comparing the three makes it easier to see what protocol-level MEV internalization is actually doing differently — and why it targets a layer none of them reach.

MEV-X Team Jun 16, 2026 6 min read
Answer first

Ethereum manages MEV through proposer-builder separation, Solana through Jito's bundle marketplace, and Sui through its object-centric execution model — all chain-level approaches to organizing who gets to compete for MEV and how. None of them decide who ultimately keeps the value a specific protocol's own activity creates. That's the layer MEV internalization operates on instead.

Ethereum: proposer-builder separation

Ethereum's answer to MEV is proposer-builder separation (PBS). Specialized builders compete to assemble the most valuable possible block — ordering transactions, including profitable bundles, extracting what MEV they can — and validators (proposers) simply pick the most valuable block offered to them, without needing to understand or extract MEV themselves. PBS was designed to stop validators from having to run sophisticated MEV extraction infrastructure just to stay competitive, by separating "who builds the block" from "who proposes it." It organizes the competition for MEV; it doesn't eliminate the competition or decide where the captured value ultimately lands beyond the builder and proposer.

Solana: the Jito marketplace

Solana's dominant MEV infrastructure runs through Jito, which operates a bundle marketplace: searchers submit atomic bundles of transactions along with a tip, and validators running Jito's client include the bundles that pay the most. It's structurally similar to Ethereum's builder market in spirit — an auction that organizes external competition for block space and ordering — adapted to Solana's much faster block times and different execution model.

Sui: MEV inside an object-centric model

Sui's object-centric execution model changes the shape of the problem rather than adding a marketplace on top of it. Because Sui tracks ownership and access at the object level rather than treating the whole chain state as one shared resource, many transactions that would create ordering-dependent MEV opportunities on an account-based chain can execute independently and in parallel instead. That doesn't eliminate MEV — opportunities that genuinely depend on shared state, like AMM pools, still exist — but it narrows where ordering-based extraction is even possible in the first place.

What all three have in common

PBS, Jito, and Sui's execution model all operate at the chain or infrastructure layer. They decide how competition for MEV is organized, who's allowed to participate in extracting it, and in some cases how much of the opportunity even exists to be extracted. What none of them decide is what happens to the value once a specific protocol's own trading activity is the thing that created it. A validator running PBS or a Jito-enabled client gets paid regardless of which AMM's swap generated the arbitrage opportunity that produced the winning bundle.

Where protocol-level internalization fits

This is the layer MEV internalization targets instead: not the chain's rules for organizing competition, but whether a specific protocol captures the value its own activity creates before that value ever becomes a target for the chain-level auction at all. Homelander operates here — resolving a swap's post-trade arbitrage atomically, inside the protocol's own transaction, before it's visible to any builder, searcher, or validator-level marketplace. It's not a competing approach to PBS or Jito; it's a different layer of the same problem, and one none of the chain-level models were designed to reach.

What is proposer-builder separation (PBS)?

Proposer-builder separation is Ethereum's model for managing MEV, in which specialized builders compete to construct the most valuable block and validators (proposers) simply select the winning block, without needing to capture MEV themselves.

How does Solana's Jito marketplace handle MEV?

Jito operates a bundle marketplace on Solana where searchers submit atomic bundles of transactions with a tip to validators, competing for inclusion. It's an auction-based model similar in spirit to Ethereum's builder market, adapted to Solana's execution environment.

What makes MEV internalization different from these chain-level models?

Chain-level models like PBS and Jito organize competition for MEV among external parties — they decide how the auction works, not who ultimately keeps the value. MEV internalization instead lets the protocol whose activity creates the opportunity capture it directly, keeping the value inside the protocol rather than routing it through an external auction at all.
